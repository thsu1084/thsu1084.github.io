SetPieceRAG

Domain-Specific RAG for Knowledge-Intensive Soccer VQA with Large Language Models

Youngseon Kim · Jongmin Lee
Chung-Ang University
Paper ↗ Poster ↗

Abstract

Visual Question Answering in soccer goes beyond recognizing what is directly visible in a frame. Many questions require player identity, career history, team context, historical statistics, and other long-tail knowledge that is absent from the image itself and often underrepresented in model parameters. SetPieceRAG addresses this gap with a domain-specific Retrieval-Augmented Generation framework designed for soccer VQA.

Beyond the core retrieval pipeline, the framework introduces task-specific adaptations including LLM ensembling, LoRA-based domain adaptation, super-resolution preprocessing, and object-centric analysis. For knowledge-intensive questions, CLIP-based visual grounding is connected to external knowledge from SoccerWiki and web-enabled multimodal models. By retrieving and injecting the evidence required by each question type, SetPieceRAG substantially improves answer accuracy on the SoccerNet VQA benchmark.

Motivation

A broadcast frame may reveal a face, kit, number, or match context, but it rarely contains the factual answer to questions such as a player's youth clubs, previous transfers, historical appearances, or career statistics. This creates a mismatch between visual evidence and the knowledge required to answer. SetPieceRAG treats soccer VQA as a grounding-and-retrieval problem rather than forcing a single model to memorize the entire football world.

Method

01

Visual Grounding

CLIP-based retrieval maps the visual query to the relevant player or entity before factual retrieval.

02

Domain Retrieval

SoccerWiki and web knowledge supply non-parametric, long-tail facts that are not visible in the broadcast.

03

Task-Adaptive Routing

Different question types trigger RAG, LoRA, super-resolution, commentary reasoning, or multimodal fallback.

Ablation & Analysis

Retrieval is most valuable when the visual signal identifies the entity but cannot answer the factual question. The test analysis contains many such cases: image-only reasoning often recognizes a kit or player context yet explicitly reports that career history or statistics are unavailable from pixels. Adding retrieved SoccerWiki evidence frequently turns these unanswerable cases into grounded answers.

Qualitative Examples

Youth clubs

An image-only model cannot recover a player's youth history, while retrieved player information provides the exact sequence of clubs.

Career statistics

Questions about appearances or goals require factual records that are not encoded in the visible frame.

Transfers & dates

Grounding the player first lets the system retrieve the relevant career timeline and answer date-specific questions.

Efficiency

The system avoids sending every query through the most expensive reasoning path. Task-specific routing and fallback allow lightweight components to handle easy or well-grounded cases, while harder questions are escalated selectively. The measured average end-to-end latency is 16.93 seconds in the reported pipeline.
